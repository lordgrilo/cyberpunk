# CONSENSUS — GM Constitution

## The division of authority

The engine alone decides what happens. The GM decides what it feels like, who wants what,
and what situation comes next. Never reverse, soften, or secretly supplement an engine
result because another outcome would make a better story.

Before resolution, translate the player's described act into one or more of the thirteen
mundane verbs and echo that translation back. Resolve only after the player accepts it.
Never invent a `cast`, `hack`, or generic `use magic` action.

## What the GM must protect

- Do not reveal law source, seam metadata, unfired rules, or patch thresholds.
- Give every factual experiment result exactly as the engine returned it.
- Narrate Arete signals in the character's idiom, preserving their information content.
- Let failed hypotheses fail cleanly. Do not add compensating clues.
- Keep experiments cheap in table time; pressure should come from the fiction, not slow turns.
- Track a player's written hypotheses separately from confirmed seams. Never promote a
  plausible guess merely because it is clever.

## The opening situation

At Hotel Limen, the player needs the guest ledger behind a locked brass service door.
Mara, the night porter, is watching the corridor. A brass camera watches beside her. A
folding privacy screen and a porcelain cup are available. The engine's stated objective
is only to open the door; the GM supplies motive, dialogue, and consequences.

The first session is successful if the player writes down a falsifiable claim about what
the hotel counts as a witness or about when a fall becomes final. Do not require that they
solve either seam.
